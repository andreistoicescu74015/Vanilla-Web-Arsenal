Flexbox in ten minutes

The two axes, the five properties you use every day, and the one-line fix for overflowing children.

AP

Ana Popescu

Sep 12, 2026 · 10 min read

Flexbox lays out items in one direction at a time: a row or a column. That single idea explains almost every property, so we start there.

Two axes

The main axis runs in the direction of flex-direction. With the default row, it goes left to right. The cross axis is perpendicular to it.

justify-content always works on the main axis and align-items on the cross axis. Change the direction to column and the two properties swap what they look like they do.

Tip

Practice with the interactive playground in tools/flexbox.html.

The five properties you use every day

  1. display: flex turns the children into flex items.
  2. gap adds space between items, never at the edges.
  3. justify-content distributes leftover space on the main axis.
  4. align-items positions items on the cross axis.
  5. flex-wrap: wrap lets items move to the next line.
.navbar {
  display: flex;
  align-items: center;
  justify-content: space-between;
  gap: 1rem;
}

Grow, shrink and basis

Each item has a starting size (flex-basis), a share of the extra space it may take (flex-grow), and a share of the missing space it gives up (flex-shrink). The shorthand flex: 1 means "start from zero and share equally".

The overflow fix

A flex item will not shrink below the width of its content by default. A long URL or a wide table then pushes the layout off the screen.

Add min-width: 0 to the flex item that should be allowed to shrink.

In the arsenal, that is the .min-w-0 utility.

Where to go next

Once rows and columns feel natural, learn grid for two-dimensional layouts. The two work together: grid for the page, flexbox for the pieces inside it.
